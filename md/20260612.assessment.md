## The Core Findings (Pilot Sample)

### Finding 1: hp_sep does not decay with distance

Between-haplotype methylation difference (hp_sep) is ~3.5x higher than non-genetic loci across **all** distance classes — proximal, distal, and very-distal. The expected decay with distance from the causal SNP does not occur. This challenges the simple model of "cis-eQTL-like" methylation effects that fall off monotonically.

**Interpretation candidates:**
- Long-range ASM captured by these loci reflects large epigenetic domains (imprinted regions, TAD-bounded allelic domains, CTCF loops)
- Selection bias: only the strongest effects survive the dASM calling threshold at long distance
- These are truly cis-genotype effects with long reach via chromatin structure

**What would distinguish these:** Overlap with ICRs and CTCF/TAD boundaries. Imprinted loci should show hp_var_asymmetry (one allele deterministic, other variable).

### Finding 2: Genotype-driven ASM is deterministic

`within_hp_entropy` is significantly lower for all genotype classes vs non-genetic. Reads within each haplotype agree with each other — the methylation state is consistent across read observations. Non-genetic methylation shows higher within-haplotype variability (stochastic maintenance).

This is the most biologically interesting finding because:
1. It's only detectable with long-read per-read data — bulk bisulfite cannot measure within-haplotype variability
2. It has mechanistic implications: cis-sequence effects on methylation are deterministic (allele either recruits or excludes the methylation machinery consistently), while non-genetic methylation is a stochastic process
3. The gradient across distance classes (proximal > distal > very-distal in within_hp_entropy) is counterintuitive and worth investigating further

### Finding 3: per_cpg_entropy loses significance at distal loci

The aggregate bimodality measure (`per_cpg_entropy`) is elevated only for proximal-genotype (p < 1e-26) but not for distal/very-distal classes. Yet hp_sep remains high. This means: at proximal loci, reads cleanly partition into two groups (high-methylation HP1, low-methylation HP2); at distal loci, the per-CpG methylation frequency still differs between haplotypes (hp_sep high) but the per-read bimodal structure is less clean. This is consistent with more CpG-level noise at distal loci while the haplotype mean is still shifted.

## Publishability Assessment

### Short answer: not yet, but achievable

**In current state (N=1):** Not publishable. Single-sample results are illustrative, not evidence.

**With 30 samples + ICR validation:** Potentially publishable as a short report in a genomics journal (Genome Biology, Genome Research, NAR, Nature Communications). Realistically a ~4-6 month push from now.

### What makes this interesting

The core differentiating factor is **per-read ONT methylation**. This enables things bisulfite cannot do:
- Within-haplotype entropy (requires read-level data, not CpG-level summaries)
- hp_var_asymmetry (differential stochasticity between HP1 and HP2)
- Multi-CpG epiallele patterns (methylation covariation across CpGs within a read)

No prior dASM paper I'm aware of has systematically measured within-haplotype determinism vs stochasticity as a function of genotype proximity. This is enabled by ONT and is the methodological contribution.

### The strongest paper angle

**Title direction:** "Allele-specific methylation in blood is genotype-constrained and deterministic, revealed by long-read single-molecule methylation"

**Three-panel story:**
1. Distance-stratified dASM: hp_sep preserved at distal loci; very-distal loci enriched at imprinted regions (needs to be shown)
2. Determinism axis: genotype-proximate loci have lower within-haplotype entropy; validated by known ASM loci (ICRs should have the most extreme profiles)
3. Population variation: constitutive vs variable ASM across 30 individuals; constitutive ASM co-segregates with haplotype

### Key weaknesses in current form

1. **N=1 is not enough for any statistical claim.** Everything needs to replicate in the 30-sample cohort.

2. **The classification scheme is unvalidated.** Proximal/distal/very-distal are defined by distance from the nearest het SNP, but this is a proxy. The true causal variant may be far from the nearest het SNP, or the classification may be confounded by LD structure. Need to validate with known ASM loci.

3. **No mechanistic interpretation of very-distal-genotype.** The claim that these are large epigenetic domains needs to be demonstrated, not assumed. The overlap with ICRs is critical and currently absent.

4. **The within_hp_entropy gradient with distance is counterintuitive and unexplained.** More deterministic at longer distances is the opposite of what a simple cis-genetic model predicts. This needs explanation — either it's an artifact of the dASM calling threshold, a property of imprinted regions, or a real novel finding. Without knowing which, it can't be presented as a positive result.

5. **hp_var_asymmetry metric (the imprinting signature) isn't computed yet.** This is the most biologically specific test for the long-range ASM hypothesis.

6. **No population genetics context.** 30 samples from 1000G — need to show which loci show consistent hp_sep across individuals (constitutive ASM = strong evidence of genetic effect) vs. variable hp_sep (epivariation, population-specific).

## My Critique of the Current Framing

The current proposal/pipeline framing emphasizes dASM calling as the primary output and epiallele analysis as a downstream add-on. I'd invert this.

**The dASM calls themselves aren't novel.** Generating a list of allele-specific methylation loci from ONT data has been done (Gilpatrick, van der Graaf, etc.). What's novel is the per-read epiallele structure analysis.

**The most defensible and interesting paper focuses on:**
- "What does genotype proximity tell us about the *type* of ASM?" — deterministic vs stochastic, symmetric vs asymmetric
- This framing makes the hp_var_asymmetry analysis central, not peripheral
- The very-distal-genotype + ICR connection, if borne out, is the headline result

**The risk:** If the ICR overlap is not enriched, the very-distal-genotype class doesn't have a compelling biological interpretation, and the distance-stratification analysis loses its narrative.

**The hedge:** Even without the ICR story, the determinism finding (lower within_hp_entropy at genotype-proximate loci) is still interesting and publishable with N=30 and validation against known ASM loci. It's a methodological contribution enabled by ONT even if the biology is incremental.

**Bottom line:** This is a 7/10 project idea with 30 samples. At N=1 it's a 4/10. The ICR/imprinting angle is the key bet that makes it 9/10 or back to 6/10. Run the overlap analysis as soon as W12 is done for all 30 samples.

## Population Genetics of ASM Across Ancestries

### Why ancestry matters for the classification scheme

The proximal/distal/very-distal classification is defined by distance to the nearest heterozygous SNP *in the individual being studied*. This is a critical dependency on per-individual heterozygosity, and heterozygosity is not uniformly distributed across ancestries.

African-ancestry individuals carry ~30% more heterozygous sites per genome than European-ancestry individuals (π_AFR ≈ 0.8×10⁻³ vs π_EUR ≈ 0.6×10⁻³), reflecting the greater genetic diversity maintained outside of bottlenecked non-African populations. This means:

1. **More loci classified as genotype-proximate in AFR individuals.** An AFR individual is likely to have a het SNP within 5–10 kb of any given CpG locus; an EUR individual may need to look 50–100 kb. In a single EUR pilot sample, many loci that are genuinely cis-genetically controlled appear "distal" or "very-distal" simply because the nearest visible het SNP is far away — not because the causal variant is far away.

2. **The very-distal class is ancestry-confounded.** If the pilot sample is EUR, the very-distal class is systematically inflated compared to what an AFR individual would show at the same loci. This is a classification artifact, not a biological signal. Running W12 across 30 individuals spanning AFR/EUR/EAS/SAS/AMR will reveal whether very-distal loci in one individual are proximal in another.

3. **LD structure determines apparent distance.** Even when two individuals are both het for a nearby SNP, the LD block around the causal variant differs by ancestry. In European populations, LD blocks are longer (fewer recombination events since the out-of-Africa bottleneck), so a causal variant may be in strong LD with a het SNP 50 kb away. In African populations, shorter LD blocks mean the same causal SNP is only in detectable LD with variants within 10–20 kb. The practical consequence: the same ASM locus will look "distal" in a EUR individual and "proximal" in an AFR individual even if both are het at a nearby SNP — because in the EUR case, a distant SNP tags the haplotype, while in the AFR case only a closer SNP does.

### Population-specific cis-genetic ASM

Not all ASM-driving variants are present in every population. Many SNPs that cause allele-specific methylation are:

- **Population-private** or low-frequency: absent entirely in some populations, so those populations show no ASM at that locus even if the epigenetic machinery is intact
- **Fixed in some populations**: if the ASM-driving allele went to fixation (everyone is homozygous), there's no heterozygous individual to detect the ASM. The locus may show uniform methylation across the population — not because there's no sequence effect, but because there's no standing variation
- **At different frequencies** due to drift, founder effects, or selection: a cis-genetic ASM locus that's common in EUR may be rare in EAS and absent in AFR or vice versa

This predicts **population-specific ASM** — loci where hp_sep is consistently elevated in individuals of one ancestry group but not others. This is the methylation equivalent of population-specific eQTLs, which have been described but are not well-characterized in blood. With 30 individuals spanning multiple 1000G superpopulations, you can test this directly: fit a mixed model with ancestry as a covariate and look for ASM loci with ancestry-specific hp_sep.

### Imprinting as the control: parent-of-origin ASM transcends ancestry

ICR-controlled imprinted loci are a clean internal positive control for this ancestry analysis because their methylation is parent-of-origin, not sequence-dependent. Every individual from every population has H19 methylated on the paternal allele — there is no population where the ICR is unmethylated on both alleles (barring UPD or epimutations). Yet the *classification* of ICR loci as genotype-proximate or non-genetic depends entirely on local het SNP density.

The expected pattern in a 30-sample analysis:
- ICR loci should show **consistently high hp_sep** across all 30 individuals (it's a constitutive imprint)
- The *classification* of those loci will vary: AFR individuals will classify them as proximal/distal-genotype; EUR individuals may classify some as non-genetic (no nearby het SNP)
- hp_var_asymmetry at ICR loci should be high and consistent across ancestries (one allele always deterministic, one always low)

This comparison — constitutive hp_sep at ICR loci regardless of classification — would directly demonstrate that the classification scheme is ancestry-confounded and that hp_sep itself is the more ancestry-robust signal.

### Connection to the demux/pool_design project

There is a structural parallel between the demux/pool_design heterogeneity problem and the ASM ancestry problem. In demultiplexing, the raw log-likelihood difference between top donor assignments is driven by the number of *informative SNPs* — sites where donors in the pool differ. AFR individuals contribute more unique variant sites, so AFR-containing pools are generally easier to demultiplex than EUR-only pools. The non-monotonicity observed in the real data (3/3 EUR/AFR pools not consistently best) likely reflects that informative SNP count is necessary but not sufficient — the *geometry* of donor haplotypes in the pool matters more than raw heterozygosity.

In ASM, heterozygosity enters at the intra-individual level (het sites for haplotagging) rather than the inter-individual level (unique variants for demux). But both trace back to the same underlying population genetics: nucleotide diversity, LD structure, and population allele frequencies. An AFR individual contributes more informative SNPs in both contexts — more haplotaggable phase anchors in ASM, and more unique sites separating them from other donors in demux.

The practical implication: the 1000G VCF already downloaded for demux simulations can be directly reused to predict haplotagging coverage per individual and per ancestry before running the ASM pipeline. A simple metric — expected number of het SNPs per 100 kb window per individual by ancestry — would predict which samples will have the best genotype-proximate coverage, and which loci will be systematically underclassified.

### What to do with this in the analysis

1. **Compare classification distributions by ancestry** across the 30 samples. AFR individuals should have more proximal-genotype loci than EUR individuals at matched hp_sep thresholds. If true, report it — it's a quantitative demonstration that classification is ancestry-confounded.

2. **Use hp_sep as the ancestry-robust signal** rather than classification category. For multi-sample analysis, fit hp_sep as the outcome rather than proximal/distal/very-distal category membership.

3. **Test for population-specific ASM**: identify loci where hp_sep is significantly elevated in AFR individuals but not EUR, or vice versa. These are candidates for population-enriched cis-genetic ASM driven by population-differentiated SNPs.

4. **ICR loci as cross-ancestry controls**: verify that ICR hp_sep is high and consistent across all 30 individuals regardless of ancestry. This validates that the assay works and that ancestry differences in classification are a statistical artifact, not a biological one.

5. **Leverage the demux VCF infrastructure**: the per-individual het SNP density maps already implicit in the 1000G VCFs used for demux simulations can predict haplotagging coverage per sample — potentially useful for planning the W02 haplotagging step for all 30 samples.
