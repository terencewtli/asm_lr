## 1. Defects to fix before rerunning

These are visible in the committed cell outputs, not speculation.

### D1 — F02: N50 is `NA` (integer overflow). **Blocking.**

```r
cum_bases <- cumsum(lenghts_sorted)   # integer vector
```
Output: `Warning: integer overflow in 'cumsum'` → `N50: NA bp (NA kb)`.

Integer `cumsum` overflows at 2³¹ ≈ 2.15 Gb; a 49× human genome is ~150 Gb of read bases.
Consequence: `geom_vline(xintercept = n50)` and the `annotate()` N50 label are both dropped
(hence the two "Removed 1 row containing missing values" warnings), so **F02a currently ships a
read-length histogram whose advertised N50 marker is silently absent.**

Fix: `cumsum(as.numeric(lenghts_sorted))`. Also `lenghts_sorted` → `lengths_sorted`.

Note the reported median read length is 18,424 bp, which is healthy — N50 is likely 40–60 kb.
This is a display bug, not a data problem.

### D2 — F02: `mod_rate` is degenerate (all zero). **Blocking.**

`head(reads)` shows `mod_rate = 0` for all six rows across read lengths from 920 bp to 109 kb.
F02c then fails outright:

```
Computation failed in `stat_binhex()`. Caused by error in `if (diff(ybnds) <= 0)`:
missing value where TRUE/FALSE needed
```

That error means the y variable has zero range or is all-NA — i.e. `mod_rate` has no variance
genome-wide. A per-read 5mCG rate of exactly 0 across every read is not biologically possible
(global mCG in F03 is ~75%).

Likely causes, in order of probability: integer division (`n_mod / n_cpgs` with both integers),
the MM/ML tag parser not counting modified bases and returning 0, or column misalignment in the
CSV writer. **F02b and F02c are both invalid until this is resolved**, and this matters more than
any other item here — per-read methylation rate is the foundation of the entire epiallele
contribution. If the per-read extractor is silently returning zeros, every downstream
single-molecule metric inherits it.

Add an assertion to the extraction script: `stopifnot(mean(reads$mod_rate) > 0.5)`.

### D3 — F03: haplotype fill scale doesn't match the data.

```
Warning: No shared levels found between `names(values)` of the manual scale
and the data's fill values.
```
Fires on the F3c cell and again at `ggsave`. `hap_hexes` is keyed `'1'`/`'2'`, so `chr_df$hap`

or `H1`/`H2`. Effect: F3c bars fall back to default ggplot colors with a mislabeled legend.

### D4 — F04: whole-genome SNP counts are extrapolated, not measured.

`n_snps_wg_est` is projected from chr1–11 (ratio ≈ 1.47). F04b plots the estimate on an axis
labeled "Estimated WG het SNPs". For a publication figure, count the real genome — it is cheap.
Also, F04c's title says "all 30 samples" but the underlying `per_chr` table only spans chr1–11;
the title and the data disagree.

## 2. The finding that actually matters: phasing is failing

This is not a plotting bug. Three numbers from the outputs converge:

| Source | Metric | Value |
|---|---|---|
| F03 `qc_pileup_coverage_summary` | median per-CpG coverage, hap1 / hap2 | **3× / 2×** |
| F03 | fraction of CpGs at ≥5× | **19.6%** |
| F05 `qc_phasing_per_chrom` | % of dASM loci phased | **17–19%, every chromosome** |

HG01258 is a 49× sample. Post-haplotagging that should give roughly 20–24× per haplotype, not a
median of 2–3×. Only ~20% of CpGs reach even 5× on a haplotype, and only ~18% of candidate loci
receive a haplotype assignment. `validation_plan.md` sets a QC gate of "haplotagging rate ≥ 60%" —
**the pilot sample fails its own gate by a wide margin.**

Two corroborating details:

- Mean reads per haplotype at phased loci is ~10.8, remarkably flat across chromosomes. That is
  consistent with a hard depth floor in the calling code rather than with natural coverage variation.
- Median AUC is 0.6363, 0.6358, 0.6363, 0.6369, 0.6389… across chr1–5. Agreement to four
  significant figures across chromosomes with 3–25k loci each is the signature of a statistic
  computed over a small discrete number of reads (~11 vs ~11), where AUC can only take a limited
  set of rational values. AUC is a low-resolution statistic at this depth, and 0.64 is close to
  the 0.5 null.

**Most likely cause, and the fix:** `lit_review.md` notes HG01258 uses SHAPEIT4 statistical
phasing. But `cohort_downloads.tsv` already lists assembly paths for this exact sample:

```
s3://human-pangenomics/working/HPRC/HG01258/assemblies/year1_f1_assembly_v2_genbank/
  HG01258.{paternal,maternal}.f1_assembly_v2_genbank.fa.gz
```

Assembly-backed phasing via `dipcall` against the two haplotype assemblies, then `whatshap
haplotag`, is what Zhuo et al. used (their Methods, "Long-read phasing") and what HPRC intends
these assemblies for. It should take haplotagging from ~18% to >90%. **Do this before generating
any more QC figures** — every panel in F03 and F05 is currently a picture of the phasing failure
rather than of the data.

### Knock-on effect on F03a/F03b

At a median haplotype depth of 2–3×, per-CpG `pct_mod` is quantized to {0, 33, 50, 67, 100}.
The F03a "bimodality" histogram is therefore largely **measuring read depth, not methylation
structure**, and the F03b hap1-vs-hap2 hexbin is noise piled into the corners. Both panels need a
coverage floor (≥5× per haplotype, ideally ≥10×) applied before plotting, and the floor should be
stated in the caption. Bimodality is real biology, but you cannot demonstrate it at 2×.

## 4. Proposed new analysis: short- vs. long-read ascertainment

This is the right instinct and it should be an early main figure — it justifies the platform
choice before any ASM result is presented. Two refinements to the framing.

### The correct estimand

ASM ascertainment requires a **single molecule to span both a heterozygous SNP and the CpG**.
So the limiting quantity is the joint distribution of (CpG → nearest het SNP distance) against the
fragment-length distribution. Model it as: for a molecule of length *L* covering a given CpG,
P(molecule also carries ≥1 het SNP) = 1 − exp(−L/d̄), where d̄ is the mean inter-het-SNP distance.

Using your own F04 numbers (NA18508, 5.30M genome-wide het SNPs → d̄ ≈ 585 bp for AFR; ~740–800 bp
for non-AFR):

| Platform | Effective spanned length | AFR (d̄=585) | EUR (d̄=800) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Illumina PE150 (both mates) | ~300 bp | 0.40 | 0.31 |
| Illumina, generous 500 bp fragment | 500 bp | 0.57 | 0.46 |
| ONT (your median read) | 18,424 bp | ~1.00 | ~1.00 |

So **~2.5–3.2× more ascertainable CpGs** on the phasing axis alone. Note the non-obvious result
worth reporting: the relative long-read advantage is *smaller* in AFR samples, because denser
heterozygosity lets short reads do better. That is a genuine ancestry-stratified finding and it
costs nothing to produce.

### The second axis, which is the one that can't be argued with

Phaseability alone is a ~3× argument, and a reviewer can counter that short-read studies use
population phasing and imputation rather than within-read phasing. The epiallele requirement
cannot be countered that way, because it needs *k* CpGs **on one molecule**:

At the genome-wide average of ~1 CpG per 111 bp, a 300 bp Illumina fragment carries ~3 CpGs; an
18.4 kb ONT read carries ~165. Any epiallele entropy over k ≥ 4 CpGs is **structurally
unavailable** to short reads at any sequencing depth. That is a difference in kind, not degree,
and it is specific to your contribution rather than to long reads generically.

### How to build it

Panel A: empirical distribution of CpG → nearest het SNP distance, per superpopulation, with
vertical markers at 300 bp / 500 bp / read N50.
Panel B: cumulative fraction of CpGs ascertainable vs. molecule length, one curve per
superpopulation, log x-axis.
Panel C: max achievable epiallele order (number of CpGs co-observed with ≥1 het SNP) vs. molecule
length.

Compute panels A and B **empirically from the actual VCF and reference CpG coordinates**, not from
the Poisson model above. Both CpGs (islands) and het SNPs cluster, so the exponential model is
only good for intuition — it will overstate short-read performance in CpG islands and understate
it in deserts. Use the model as a sanity check on the empirical curve, not as the figure.

**Framing caveat, state it explicitly:** this measures *direct molecular phasing without external
information*. Short-read ASM/meQTL studies legitimately use population phasing and imputation, and
GoDMC's 32,851 samples are not refuted by this figure. Scope the claim to per-molecule
ascertainment and haplotype-resolved epiallele structure. Then present V06 (the empirical
short-read simulation from `validation_plan.md`) alongside it — the theoretical curve and the
simulation answer the same question by different routes, and agreement between them is far more
convincing than either alone. They belong in the same figure.

## 5. Other analyses worth adding

**5.1 — Coverage-titration of the ASM call set.** Downsample the pilot to 10/20/30/40× and plot
loci recovered and effect-size bias vs. depth. This answers "is 50× enough?" and, given the depth
problem in §2, tells you where your own floor sits. The MDPI/IJMS 2025 trio-binning paper reports
diminishing returns past ~30×; confirming or contradicting that on your data is a cheap, citable
result. Pairs naturally with the rarefaction figure (S7) from `zhuo_2026/paper_comparison.md`.

**5.2 — Phasing-method comparison, as a figure.** Since §2 requires redoing phasing anyway,
capture SHAPEIT4-statistical vs. dipcall-assembly-backed side by side: haplotagging rate, phase
block N50, and dASM yield under each. This converts a bug fix into a methods result, and it
directly supports the distal class — statistical phasing degrades with distance, which is exactly
the confounder a reviewer will raise against your distal ASM loci.

**5.3 — Switch-error rate against the assemblies.** You have both parental assemblies for these
samples. Quantifying switch error per phase block gives a direct error bar on the distal class and
pre-empts "your distal signal is phasing noise." Nothing else in the plan measures this.

**5.4 — Imprinted loci as a positive control.** Known imprinted DMRs (H19/ICR1, KCNQ1OT1, MEST,
SNRPN, PEG3, GNAS) must show near-100% ASM with |Δ| ≈ 0.5 in every sample. This is the standard
sanity check for haplotype-resolved methylation — the 1KGP-ONT paper (Gustafson et al., *Genome
Research* 2024) validates its methylation calls this way. It is the cheapest possible demonstration
that the pipeline works, it doubles as a per-sample QC gate for V01, and its absence will be
noticed.

**5.5 — X-inactivation as a second positive control.** In female samples, chrX should show a
distinctive haplotype-methylation signature that autosomes do not. Your cohort is sex-balanced,
so this is a free internal control with a built-in negative (male samples). Also validated in the
1KGP-ONT paper.

**5.6 — 5hmC as differentiation, not just a bonus.** The R10.4.1 subset carries 5mCG+5hmCG.
Zhuo cannot address 5hmC at all — R9.4.1 and HiFi primrose do not resolve it. Even a modest panel
(is allele-specific 5hmC detectable? does it colocalize with allele-specific 5mC or anti-correlate?)
is a result nobody in this niche can currently produce, and it independently justifies restricting
to the 18 R10.4.1 samples. This may be the strongest single argument that the 18-sample cohort is
a *design* rather than a *subset*.

### Priority order

2. Redo phasing with dipcall/assemblies (§2) — everything downstream depends on it
3. Imprinted-loci positive control (5.4) — cheapest proof the pipeline works

5. Fix D1, D3, D4 — cosmetic but trivially cheap
6. 5.2, 5.3, 5.6 — the differentiating analyses, once the foundation is sound
