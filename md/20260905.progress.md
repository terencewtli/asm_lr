# ASM calling (W04 Fisher pipeline) — first-pass results, QC, and open methodological issues

**Written 2026-09-05.** Documents the first attempt to run the Fisher-based dASM calling step
(`W04_dasm_loci_per_chrom.sh` + `W04_dasm_loci.py`) across the 18-donor cohort, the bug found and
fixed along the way, and an extended QC investigation triggered by wildly inconsistent results
across samples. **Main conclusion up front: the pipeline's per-CpG Fisher+fixed-threshold approach
cannot currently support any cross-sample or cross-ancestry ASM comparison.** The variance between
samples is dominated by depth and by a handful of individually anomalous samples, not by ancestry.
Do not report an "ASM rate by population" number from this pipeline as it stands.

---

## 1. Bug fixed: BED4 vs BED6 in `modkit extract --include-bed`

`W04_dasm_loci.py` (`cmd_call_loci`) wrote the candidate-loci BED as 4 columns
(`chrom, start, end, locus_id`). `modkit extract --include-bed` requires BED3 or BED6 and rejected
every candidate file with `zero valid positions parsed from BED file`, failing 100% of tasks at
stage 2/3 before this fix. Fixed by padding to BED6 (`..., locus_id, 0, .`). This had never been
caught because the pipeline had never been run past a single killed pilot task (see `20260903_qc_review.md`
context) before this session.

## 2. First pilot: chr1, NA18508 (AFR) — implausibly large result

A 5-task pilot (chr1 + chr19–22, NA18508) validated the fix and gave real timing (call-loci: 1–4.5
min per chrom; well inside the `h_rt=4h` budget). But chr1 alone called:

- 4,362,124 CpGs tested (≥5x both haplotypes)
- 429,125 (9.84%) significant at BH-FDR<0.05 & |Δ|≥0.10
- 113,119 loci after 500bp clustering
- **Median effect size among called loci: |Δ|≈0.70** — far above the 0.10 floor, and larger than
  the effect size typically associated with true imprinted/monoallelic loci

For comparison, the deCODE Icelandic long-read ASM paper (*"The correlation between CpG methylation
and gene expression is driven by sequence variants,"* Nat Genet 2024,
[10.1038/s41588-024-01851-2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-024-01851-2)/[PMC11319203](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11319203/)),
7,179 Icelanders, ONT, mean 20.6x — the closest published long-read precedent — reports a
**final validated ASM-QTL rate of 0.51%** of tested CpG units (189,178 candidates / 1.2% →
77,789 validated / 0.51%, after Bonferroni-corrected genotype regression). Our 9.8% Stage-1
candidate rate (the closest analog to their 1.2% candidate stage) is still ~8x theirs even before
accounting for the effect-size discrepancy.

## 3. Hypotheses tested and ruled out (in order)

### 3.1 CpG-disrupting SNPs — ruled out as dominant cause
deCODE's own pipeline explicitly excludes any CpG within 5bp of a known SNP (MAF>0.001), because a
nearby variant can produce a pore signal that mimics 5mC. We had no equivalent filter. Tested
directly against NA18508's chr1 het-SNP VCF (403,632 SNPs) using deCODE's own 5bp window:

| Window | All loci | Narrow (≤2 CpG) loci | Narrow & high-Δ (≥0.9) loci |
|---|---|---|---|
| ±0bp | 20.5% | 2.8% | 1.3% |
| ±5bp | 21.9% | 4.3% | 2.8% |
| ±10bp | 23.2% | 6.0% | 4.2% |

Only a small minority of even the most extreme calls sit near a het SNP. **Worth adopting deCODE's
filter regardless (best practice), but it is not the dominant driver here.**

### 3.2 HP1/HP2 label bug — ruled out
Checked whether "HP1" vs "HP2" as pipeline labels were systematically skewed (e.g. a column-order
bug): 49.5%/50.5% direction split, near-identical mean methylation (41.9% vs 42.4%). No trivial
labeling bug. (Does not rule out a genotype/REF-vs-ALT-level bias, which wasn't tested — HP1/HP2
are arbitrary per-phase-block labels, not stably reference-matching.)

### 3.3 EUR pilot (HG00126) — the opposite anomaly
Running the identical code/thresholds on HG00126 (EUR/GBR) chr1 gave: 3,419,115 tested, **5**
significant CpGs, **2** loci — a **~56,000x** difference from NA18508 on the same chromosome, same
thresholds. Neither number looks like real biology; deCODE's own validated rate (0.51%) sits
between them. Ruled out "EUR truly has no haplotype differences" — see §3.4.

### 3.4 Depth/power confound — real, but not sufficient alone
Recomputed the full pre-filter delta distribution (not saved by the pipeline) for both samples:

| | NA18508 (AFR) | HG00126 (EUR) |
|---|---|---|
| Total coverage (mosdepth) | 88.9x | 41.1x |
| Median per-haplotype depth (tested CpGs) | 18x | 8x |
| Raw \|Δ\|≥0.10 (pre-stats) | 32.4% | **42.9%** (higher!) |
| Raw \|Δ\|≥0.50 | 9.3% | 2.0% |
| **Final significant (FDR<0.05 & Δ≥0.10)** | **9.8%** | **0.00015%** |

Raw signal is comparable (HG00126 even has more raw 0.10-floor candidates); the ~56,000x gap in
*significant* calls is overwhelmingly a **Fisher's-exact-test power artifact of depth**, not a real
difference in signal. HG00126 is very likely dramatically under-powered, not "flat." However, depth
alone is not sufficient — see §3.5.

## 4. Root-causing the "elevated" samples — extensive QC, no single cause found

Tested and ruled out, for the elevated group (NA18508, NA20762, HG01167, NA21093, NA18620,
NA20870) vs. the rest:

- **Sequencing batch/facility/date** (`csv/meta/hprc_r2_ont.csv`): all UCSC, all
  `UCSC_HPRC_nanopore_Year4` (except NA18959, which is a *low*-group sample on a different
  production round) — run dates are interleaved between elevated and low samples, not clustered.
  **Ruled out.**
- **Coverage evenness across chromosomes** (coefficient of variation of per-chrom mosdepth means):
  elevated group 0.095–0.106, low group 0.094–0.140 (NA18959, a *low* sample, has the highest CV of
  the cohort). **No pattern. Ruled out.**
- **Number of merged uBAM runs** (`n_ubam_files` / `ont_bam_files`): elevated = {3,5,3,6,4,3}, low =
  {6,3,3,6,3,3,6,3,4,3,5,3} — fully overlapping ranges. **Ruled out.**
- **Manifest notes column**: no flagged QC concerns for any of the 6; annotations are generic
  ("YRI critical diversity," "highest SAS coverage," etc.), not quality warnings. **Ruled out.**
- **External documentation** (1000 Genomes QC/exclusion lists, HPRC known-issues docs, GitHub
  errata): none of the 6 sample IDs (NA18508, NA20762, HG01167, NA21093, NA18620, NA20870) appear
  on any documented 1000G relatedness/contamination/sex-mismatch exclusion list, nor in any
  HPRC-specific QC report found. **This is a genuine "not found," not a clean bill of health** —
  neither consortium appears to publish a per-sample searchable QC registry at this granularity, so
  absence of a hit is weak evidence either way.
- **Haplotagging rate**: spot-checked NA18508 (chr20: 38.4% tagged; chr1: 34.3% tagged, from
  1,203,293 mapped reads). A full 18-sample chr1 comparison was started but a direct BAM
  recomputation is slow (large text-format SAM parsing); partial recovery from historical W01
  logs found NA21093 (elevated) and NA18620 (elevated) chr1 HP-tag counts but no low-group
  comparison point yet. **Inconclusive — not yet ruled out, most promising remaining lead.**

**Bottom line: after ruling out batch, facility, date, coverage evenness, run-count, manifest
annotations, and external QC documentation, no single explanatory variable has been identified for
why 6 specific samples (spread across all 5 ancestry groups) show anomalously elevated ASM-like
signal. Haplotagging/phasing-block quality remains the most likely untested lead** — worth a
proper follow-up (phase block N50, % reads with an HP tag, per-chromosome not just per-sample).

## 5. Reference bias and CpG completeness

- NA18508 (AFR) has ~1M more "tested" CpGs than HG00126 (EUR) on chr1 (4.36M vs 3.42M), and ~1.95M
  more CpG positions with *any* coverage at all (8.78M vs 6.83M). Real inter-individual CpG-site
  differences (from CpG-creating/destroying SNPs) are on the order of thousands per chromosome, not
  millions — **this gap is much more likely a sequencing-depth-saturation effect** (more total
  reads reach more of the genome's harder-to-map positions) than "AFR genomes have a million more
  real CpGs." Not yet decisively tested — the clean experiment is to downsample NA18508 to
  HG00126's depth and see whether the gap closes.
- Independent literature support that reference choice measurably affects CpG completeness: a
  T2T-CHM13 vs. GRCh38 comparison found T2T calls 7.4% more CpGs genome-wide across four
  short-read methylation methods, and a pangenome reference finds a further 4.5% beyond either
  single linear reference, concentrated in population-specific regions
  ([PMC12288590](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12288590/)). This supports the
  *direction* of a reference-bias effect but is not itself large enough to explain a ~1M-CpG,
  ~28% gap on one chromosome for one sample pair — treat as directionally supportive context, not
  a quantitative explanation.

## 7. Literature context (population genetics × methylation)

- Ancestry-associated global methylation differences are reported in blood
  (Zhang 2011; Galanter et al. 2017 *eLife*, [10.7554/eLife.20532](https://elifesciences.org/articles/20532),
  which explicitly attributes effects to a mix of genetic ancestry *and* environmental exposure,
  not cleanly separable in admixed US cohorts).
- The meQTL field's ancestry-portability work is real but an order of magnitude smaller than
  GWAS-scale work: GoDMC (27,750 samples, ~all European,
  [godmc.org.uk](http://www.godmc.org.uk/)) is the dominant resource; an East Asian meQTL study
  (3,523 individuals, [Nat Genet 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-023-01494-9)) found
  >9% EAS-specific effects vs. GoDMC; GENOA (961 African-American samples,
  [PMC10175543](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175543/)) found only 76–93% meQTL
  cross-ancestry replication, attributed to allele-frequency/LD differences — the same mechanism as
  GWAS/PRS portability loss.
- **Long-read/HPRC-specific ancestry-stratified methylation analysis appears to be open ground.**
  HPRC2 built the infrastructure (panepigenome, local-ancestry estimates) but no published
  ancestry-stratified long-read ASM/methylation finding was found. A related long-read RNA-seq
  paper found ancestry bias in gene annotation on HPRC-adjacent data
  ([Nat Commun 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-66096-x)) — same bias class,
  different molecule.
- No paper was found isolating reference-mapping bias as a cause of shifted *global/bulk*
  methylation percentage by ancestry (as opposed to locus-specific ASM/meQTL bias, which is
  covered). Real gap.
- No paper found reporting depth-confounded ASM detection specifically for long-read methylation;
  the analogous, well-established problem in allele-specific *expression* (RNA-seq) is corrected
  for with beta-binomial models and tools like WASP (van de Geijn et al. 2015) and MBASED — WASP
  specifically discards reads that fail to remap identically after allele-swapping, trading depth
  for bias-removal; no clean universal "% coverage recovered" figure exists in the literature for
  it.
- Old single-donor (HG01258) pipeline precedent: overlapping all 304,514 genome-wide dASM loci
  against 9 canonical imprinted DMRs found **imprinting explains <0.1% of "non-genetic" loci** —
  i.e. even the field's own true positive-control biology (imprinting) does not produce large call
  volumes, reinforcing that large ASM-like call counts (whether in the old pipeline or the new
  NA18508 chr1 result) are unlikely to be dominated by real biology.

## 8. Methodological recommendation (not yet implemented)

The core problem: per-CpG Fisher's exact test + a fixed effect-size floor (0.10) + BH-FDR conflates
"no data" (low depth) with "no difference," and provides no way to compare samples of unequal
power. Recommended redesign, roughly in priority order:

1. **Root-cause the 6 elevated samples** before any further statistical work — most likely lead is
   haplotagging/phasing-block quality, not yet conclusively tested (see §4).
2. **Replace the custom Fisher+floor test with an established depth-aware method** — DSS
   (`dmlTest`/`callDML`, built-in shrinkage and smoothing) or methylKit (logistic regression with
   depth/covariate weighting) — rather than patching the existing script incrementally.
3. **Report effect size + confidence interval per locus**, not pass/fail counts — low-power samples
   should show wide CIs, not silently vanish to zero (exactly what happened to HG00126 above).
4. **Per-sample power analysis**: minimum detectable effect size at 80% power, genome-wide FDR<0.05,
   given each sample's actual depth distribution — report this alongside any count, so "5 CpGs
   significant" becomes "powered only to detect Δ>0.35," an honest and very different claim.
5. **Subsampling-replicability for high-depth samples**: repeatedly subsample to a common target
   depth and require a locus to replicate across most draws to call it robust — converts "high
   depth finds more" from a confound into supporting evidence.
6. **Hierarchical/mixed model for any real ancestry-level claim** (sample + depth as random effect,
   ancestry as fixed effect) — only justified once 1–5 make individual sample calls trustworthy.

# Part 2 — same day, continued: root-causing sample anomalies, a new ASM caller, first real result

Everything below happened later the same day, after the QC/root-cause work above. Main
conclusion up front: **the per-CpG statistical approach (Fisher, and repurposed DSS) is
retired in favor of a purpose-built beta-binomial region-pooling method, now validated in
simulation and run once successfully on real data (HG00146, chr1: 249/204,284 regions
significant, 0.12% — a sane number, finally).** Separately, the root cause of several
"elevated"/anomalous samples turned out to be a real, fixed pipeline bug (silently
truncated alignments), not a statistical artifact.

## 10. Root cause of 6 "incomplete" samples: a real bug in `W00a_align_ubam.sh`, not just bad luck

Cross-checked expected raw uBAM file sizes (`csv/meta/W15_download_urls.tsv`, from HPRC's
own manifest) against what actually got merged (`W00b_merge_wg` logs) for all 18 donors.
Six samples (HG00126, NA20762, HG00253, NA19776, HG03784, NA20870) retained only 52-80% of
their expected data. Checking the raw archived data (uBAMs were genozip-compressed for
space, not deleted): **22 of the 23 affected files have healthy, complete-looking genozip
archives** — the raw download was fine. Only one file (HG00126's first run) has a literal
0-byte archive.

Found the actual mechanism in `W00a_align_ubam.sh`'s old skip-check:
```
if [ -f "$ALIGNED" ] && [ -s "$ALIGNED" ]; then  # only checks non-empty, not complete
```
A job killed mid-alignment (walltime/OOM/node failure) leaves a truncated-but-validly-
formatted BAM. Confirmed directly in a historical log: a later "fix" attempt found an 11G
output (vs. ~69G expected), ran `samtools quickcheck` (checks BGZF format validity, not
completeness), saw it "PASSED," and left it alone — silently locking in the truncation on
every subsequent resubmission.

**Fixed** in `scripts/wg/W00a_align_ubam.sh`: now counts source reads via `samtools view -c`,
counts aligned reads, and fails loudly (removing the `.done` marker) if aligned/source <
90%, instead of silently succeeding. Wrote `scripts/wg/W00c_fix_truncated_alignments.sh`
to recover the 23 affected files (genounzip the 22 intact archives; re-download only the
one 0-byte one from HPRC S3) — submitted (by the user) and essentially complete (22/23
done as of last check).

**Important scope note**: this explains 2 of the 6 originally-flagged "elevated" samples
(NA20762, NA20870) and separately explains why 4 *different* samples (HG00126, HG00253,
NA19776, HG03784) sat in the "low/suppressed" group — consistent with the depth-power
mechanism already documented above. **It does NOT explain the other 4 originally-flagged
elevated samples (NA18508, HG01167, NA21093, NA18620), which have complete downloads.**
That remains open — see §4 above. A weak, non-explanatory correlate was found:
chr1 haplotagging rate averaged 35.8% for the elevated group vs. 33.2% for the rest, but
with clear counterexamples (NA19700 has one of the highest tagging rates yet is in the
low-candidate group), so this is a lead, not an answer.

## 11. Phasing gap: the pipeline has never used the ONT reads' own phasing information

Checked precisely what `W01_phase_per_chrom.sh` does: it calls `whatshap haplotag` only.
The het-SNP VCFs it tags against already have `|`-phased genotypes pulled directly from
the 1000 Genomes 3202-sample statistically-phased panel (NYGC). **`haplotag` only assigns
reads to a pre-existing phase — it never runs `whatshap phase`, which would use the long
reads' own multi-SNP physical linkage to refine or correct that phase.** Despite using
long-read data throughout, haplotype assignment currently owes nothing to the reads'
actual phasing power.

Literature: hybrid phasing (statistical panel as prior + read-based refinement) cuts
switch error 3-6x vs. either method alone. Separately, population-panel phasing is
measurably worse for higher-diversity/less-panel-represented ancestries (one benchmark:
~4x higher switch error, >8x higher genotype error, for an African-ancestry dataset vs.
European) — a real, literature-supported contributor to the AFR-sample anomalies, on top
of whatever else is going on.

Built `scripts/wg/W01b_phase_readbased_per_chrom.sh` (`whatshap phase`, taking the
existing phased VCF and the ONT BAM together, native `--chromosome` support matching the
existing per-chrom pattern). **Timed on real data, not yet run at scale**: HG00146/chr21
(smallest chromosome) took 6m35s; extrapolating by chromosome size gives ~35 min for chr1,
~18 min average per (sample, chromosome) — comfortably within budget for the full
18×22 array whenever it's prioritized.

## 12. New ASM caller: `ont_asm_caller` (separate repo, github.com/terencewtli/ont_asm_caller)

Retired the per-CpG Fisher approach after finding it has **77-90% empirical FDR** in a
controlled simulation with known ground truth (not just inferred from real-data anomalies)
— it is not merely underpowered, it is actively unsafe. Also retired the idea of
repurposing DSS: DSS's dispersion parameter is estimated from between-*replicate*
variance, which is undefined for a single individual's two haplotypes (not replicates) —
this is the likely reason DSS hung indefinitely (25+ min, no progress) when tried on real
project data.

**The model** (`ont_asm_caller/model.py`): per-locus beta-binomial likelihood-ratio test
(`H0: mu1=mu2` vs `H1: mu1!=mu2`), with the overdispersion parameter estimated **once,
genome-wide, pooling across loci** rather than replicates (`ont_asm_caller/dispersion.py`)
— loci substitute for the replicates a normal DMR tool would use, since there are millions
of them and zero of the other.

**Region-level pooling** (`ont_asm_caller/region.py`) was needed because the per-CpG test,
while correctly calibrated in isolation, has near-zero power once BH-FDR is applied at a
realistic genome-wide scale (~0.5% true ASM prevalence, per deCODE's validated rate).
Clusters CpGs within 500bp (matching this project's `GAP_MERGE_BP` and DSS's
`smoothing.span`), pools counts, tests once per region. **Two real bugs found and fixed
during development, both caught by writing simulations rather than trusting the design
on paper:**
1. Pooling several independent per-CpG beta-binomial draws *lowers* the effective
   dispersion of the combined count (averaging out site-specific noise) — reusing the
   per-CpG rho estimate for pooled counts is misspecified and made the region test
   uselessly conservative. Fix: re-estimate dispersion on the pooled region-level counts.
2. A simple adjacent-gap merge rule can daisy-chain through any CpG-dense stretch into an
   absurd "region" — found on real chr1 data with no cap: median 20 CpGs, but a tail
   reaching **21,163 CpGs spanning 335kb in one region**. Fixed with a `max_span=1000`
   cap forcing a split regardless of individual gaps. Real chr1 result after the fix:
   204,284 regions, median 16 CpGs, 99th percentile 104, max span exactly 1000bp.

**Benchmark** (two-layer mixture simulation: existence, π=0.5%, + effect size given
existence; `benchmarks/compare_methods.py`):

| Depth | Method | n called | Empirical FDR | Power | Effect-size bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18x | Fisher (original) | 365 | 0.822 | 0.168 | +0.249 |
| 18x | Beta-binomial, per-CpG | 1 | 0.000 | 0.003 | +0.479 |
| 18x | **Beta-binomial, region** | **45** | **0.111** | **0.615** | **+0.016** |
| 8x | Fisher | 0 | — | 0.000 | — |
| 8x | Beta-binomial, per-CpG | 0 | — | 0.000 | — |
| 8x | **Beta-binomial, region** | **26** | **0.077** | **0.369** | **+0.044** |
| 5-40x | Fisher | 1256 | 0.898 | 0.274 | +0.190 |
| 5-40x | Beta-binomial, per-CpG | 7 | 0.143 | 0.013 | +0.346 |
| 5-40x | **Beta-binomial, region** | **56** | **0.107** | **0.649** | **+0.012** |

Region pooling is a real fix, not yet a finished one: power goes from ~0-1% to 37-65%,
FDR (8-11%) is categorically better than Fisher's (77-90%) but not yet at the 5% nominal
target, and effect-size bias (+0.01-0.04) is far better than Fisher's (+0.19-0.25).

**Literature context**: the beta-binomial-with-pooled-dispersion device is well-established
for allele-specific *expression* (MBASED, QuASAR, RASQUAL) — this ports it to methylation.
For methylation ASM specifically, deCODE's own initial calling is a bare rule-based
threshold, not a statistical test at all (rigor comes from a downstream population-scale
genotype regression across 7,179 genomes — a substitution not available at n=18). The
closest real single-individual precedent found is **CPEL** (Jiang et al., *Nat Commun*
2020) — an Ising-model approach jointly modeling methylation-level and entropy imbalance
within a haplotype, more statistically sophisticated about within-region CpG correlation
than this package's simple count-pooling, and worth benchmarking against directly. Net
field assessment: real but fragmented (CPEL, ASMS, MethHaplo, mHapTk, SNPsplit,
CGmapTools, MONOD2, MethPipe exist) — nowhere near DSS/methylKit-level standardization for
single-sample ASM specifically. Also: the field's depth discussions are about *absolute*
coverage sufficiency, not *cross-sample calibration* — the >50,000x candidate-rate swing
this project measured is this project's own empirical contribution, not something the
literature already centrally names.

## 13. First real-data result

Computational cost, timed on real data (HG00146, chr1, 4.35M tested CpGs): I/O+merge 35s,
dispersion estimation <1s, clustering 3s, region testing ~16 min → **~17 min total per
(sample, chromosome)**. Very tractable; all 8 good donors × chr1 runs in parallel in about
the same wall-clock time as one.

**HG00146, chr1, region-level beta-binomial**: 204,284 regions tested, rho_hat=0.0179,
**249 significant (0.12%)** — a sane, plausible number, in the neighborhood of (actually
somewhat below) deCODE's validated 0.51% genome-wide rate, and worlds away from the
original Fisher pipeline's 9.8%/113,119-loci result on NA18508's chr1. First real evidence
the new method behaves sensibly outside of simulation.

Submitted the remaining 7 good donors (`qsub scripts/wg/good_donors/G02_betabinom_region_chr1.sh`,
job 14684888) — results pending as of this writing.
