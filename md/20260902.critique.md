# Chat Summary: Publishability & Validation of Long-Read ASM/HPRC Project

## Context
Follow-up discussion evaluating a long-read allele-specific methylation (ASM) project
(within-haplotype methylation heterogeneity, ~18–30 HPRC donors) against a newly
published Genome Research paper on TE insertion methylation in HPRC pangenome data.

## 1. Does the Genome Research TE-methylation paper validate the project?
- **Paper:** "Characterizing cytosine methylation of polymorphic transposable element
  insertions using human pangenome resources" (Genome Research, early access, May 2026).
- Confirms long-read methylation + HPRC pangenome resources is an active, publishable
  research axis (validated long-read vs. WGBS concordance, published in a solid venue).
- Does **not** directly validate the specific novel claim (within-haplotype heterogeneity /
  dASM independent of SNP phase) — different underlying question (TE insertion context vs.
  epigenetic variance conditional on haplotype). Useful as an intro citation / field-support
  reference, not as direct evidence for the specific hypothesis.

## 2. Basecaller mismatch (Guppy vs. Dorado, 12 vs. 18 donors)
- Flagged as a real methodological risk, not just inconvenience — different 5mC calling
  models can introduce systematic batch effects indistinguishable from biology.
- Recommendation: rebasecall the 12 with Dorado from raw signal (POD5/FAST5) if retrievable;
  otherwise exclude and explicitly note as a limitation.

## 3. Power scaling, 18 → 30 donors
- Crude √n scaling: √(30/18) ≈ 1.29 → ~29% improvement in detectable effect size for a
  **fixed-effect** test. Not a dramatic gain under that framework.
- But ASM discovery is more naturally a **rarefaction/accumulation-curve** question
  ("how many recurrent loci exist, have I found most of them") than a classical power
  calculation.
- Suggested approach: subsample n = 2,4,6,...,18 from existing donors (multiple random
  draws per n), plot cumulative unique ASM loci discovered, fit a saturating curve
  (Michaelis-Menten or Chao-style estimator) to extrapolate expected loci at n=30.
  Upset plots show *which* loci overlap across specific donors; the accumulation curve
  answers the saturation question more directly.

## 4. Literature check: has ASM discovery-vs-cohort-size saturation been done before?
- No paper found doing a formal saturation/rarefaction analysis of ASM loci discovery
  vs. cohort size, in either short-read or long-read data — appears to be a genuine gap.
- Closest adjacent findings:
  - A long-read ASM study found discovery saturates with **per-individual coverage**
    above ~30x — a different axis (depth, not cohort size).
  - Autism long-read methylome study (n=31 probands, Science Advances) reports finding
    "multiple recurrent" ASM/oASM loci across unrelated probands as informal robustness
    evidence, but does not quantify a discovery curve.
  - Classical EWAS/GWAS power papers model per-locus statistical power for a fixed
    hypothesis, not cumulative discovery — a different question.
  - A pangenome + SV methylation paper (GA4K + 94 HPRC assemblies, 435 methylomes)
    is the closest HPRC-scale precedent but does not present a saturation curve either.
- Implication: doing this analysis rigorously (many random subsample draws per n) could be
  a genuine methodological contribution in its own right, not just an internal QC step —
  but there's no external benchmark curve to sanity-check against, so the resampling needs
  to be careful and well-documented.

## Open items / next steps
- Decide on Dorado rebasecalling vs. exclusion + limitation note for the 12 donors.
- Run subsampling/saturation curve analysis on existing 18 donors before deciding whether
  to prioritize compute burden of expanding to 30.
- Consider explicitly citing the saturation-curve gap as a secondary contribution in the
  eventual manuscript.
