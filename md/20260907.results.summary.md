# A first cross-donor ASM locus set: replication, imprinting, and meQTL enrichment

**2026-09-07.** First pass at a finalized, cross-donor-replicated allele-specific methylation
(ASM) locus list from this project's 18-donor HPRC ONT long-read cohort, and the quick discovery
checks against it. Scope: the 6 donors already confirmed free of the anomalous candidate-rate
behavior described elsewhere (`md/20260907_phasing_qc_review.md`) — HG00146, HG02392, NA18959,
NA19682, NA19700, NA21110.

## Finalization rule

An explicitly arbitrary first-pass definition, not a methodologically final one: merge each
donor's significant beta-binomial regions genome-wide (`bedtools merge`, 250bp slop to absorb
boundary jitter between donors' independently-clustered regions), and keep merged loci
supported by **at least 2 of the 6 donors**.

- 38,245 significant regions pooled across the 6 donors, all 22 autosomes
- merge into 28,255 distinct genomic loci
- **3,821 loci (13.5%) are supported by ≥2 of 6 donors — the finalized set**

## Result 1 — proximal/distal composition is stable under finalization

![category breakdown](/u/project/cluo/terencew/claude/project_ideas/asm_lr/tables/phasing_qc/fig_category_breakdown.png)

| Category | n loci | % |
|---|---|---|
| Proximal (≤200bp to nearest het SNP) | 3,489 | 91.3% |
| Distal (200bp–5kb) | 315 | 8.2% |
| Very distal (>5kb) | 17 | 0.4% |

Matches the pre-finalization, per-donor-pooled breakdown from the earlier ASM01 analysis
(91.9%/7.4%/0.7%) closely — requiring cross-donor replication doesn't shift the proximal/distal
mix, which is a mild point in favor of both sets reflecting the same underlying phenomenon
rather than the replication filter selecting a qualitatively different population of loci.

## Result 2 — effect sizes

![effect size](/u/project/cluo/terencew/claude/project_ideas/asm_lr/tables/phasing_qc/fig_effect_size.png)

Effect size (|Δ| methylation between haplotypes, best-supporting donor per locus) is
concentrated at large values, consistent with genuine allele-specific methylation rather than
borderline/noise-level calls surviving by chance.

## Result 3 — cross-donor replication landscape

![replication histogram](/u/project/cluo/terencew/claude/project_ideas/asm_lr/tables/phasing_qc/fig_replication_hist.png)

Distribution of donor support across all 28,255 merged loci (before the ≥2-donor filter) —
most loci are donor-private (consistent with the ~30% background replication rate already
characterized), with a long tail supported by more donors; the finalized set is everything at
or past the ≥2 mark.

## Result 4 — known imprinted loci: ~49% recovered by replication alone

Checked the finalized (≥2-donor) locus set against the Zink et al. 2018 genome-wide
parent-of-origin DMR set (229 loci): **112 of 229 (48.9%) are hit**, using only the ≥2-donor
replication criterion — no imprinting-specific tuning at all. This is an independent, orthogonal
validation of the finalization rule: imprinted loci are expected to replicate near-universally
across unrelated individuals (parent-of-origin driven, not genotype-frequency-dependent), and
they do, at a rate far above the ~30% background.

## Result 5 — monocyte meQTL enrichment, ~2.6×

![meQTL enrichment](/u/project/cluo/terencew/claude/project_ideas/asm_lr/tables/phasing_qc/fig_meqtl_enrichment.png)

Of the finalized loci with a 450K array probe within 250bp (1,381 of 3,821, 36.1% — array
coverage is sparse relative to long-read CpG density, so this is expected), **31.1% carry a
probe that's also a significant monocyte meQTL** (`data/meqtl_mono_sig_cpgs.txt`, from the
phase-1 W08 analysis), against an **11.8% genome-wide background rate** for all 450K probes —
roughly **2.6× enrichment**. A second independent, orthogonal signal that the finalized set is
biologically real, not merely an artifact of the replication-filter mechanics.

## Reading these results together

Two genuinely independent validations (imprinting recovery, meQTL enrichment) both show strong
positive signal for the same ≥2-donor-replicated locus set, using data and methodology
(imprinted DMRs, 450K meQTL) that have nothing to do with how the loci were called. That's
reassuring at the aggregate/positive-control level. It does **not** resolve the open,
locus-level calibration question from the same day's other investigation
(`md/20260907_phasing_qc_review.md` §6): the beta-binomial caller has a confirmed, real
region-pooling pseudoreplication defect, of unknown per-locus severity, that could still inflate
the false-positive rate within this set at the individual-locus level even while the aggregate
signal looks real. The read-level confirmatory pass (`P06_readlevel_confirm.py`, wired in the
same day, running as of this writing) is the intended follow-up to give per-locus confidence
rather than only aggregate confidence.

## What generated quickly vs. what didn't

Everything above ran in a few minutes, because it reuses data that already existed
(`tables/asm_analysis/six_donor_sig_regions.tsv`, the Zink DMR set, the 450K probe BED and
monocyte meQTL list from phase 1) — no new sequencing, alignment, or genome-scale recalling was
needed. The read-level confirmatory pass is the slow part: it requires a fresh, full linear scan
of each donor's multi-gigabyte `modkit extract` output per chromosome (not indexed by position),
which is minutes-to-tens-of-minutes per (sample, chromosome) — genome-wide across 6 donors × 22
chromosomes is a real, if mechanical, compute job, not a quick add-on.

See `md/20260907_outstanding_items.md` for the full punch list this result set still depends on
or should be checked against before anything here is presented as final.
